## 1. Project Identity

**Working title:** GeoThermAI — AI-Powered Geothermal Exploration Decision Support System for Thailand

**Research domain:** Geothermal energy, geospatial machine learning, satellite data fusion, uncertainty quantification, exploration decision support.

**One-sentence mission:**

Develop a scientifically defensible, uncertainty-aware geospatial AI framework to prioritize geothermal exploration areas in Thailand and support early-stage exploration decisions.

## 2. Problem Statement

Geothermal exploration requires decisions under substantial geological uncertainty. Subsurface conditions cannot be established from surface observations alone. Detailed geophysical surveys and drilling may be costly.

Geological maps, fault information, hot spring records, satellite observations, and other spatial datasets may provide useful screening evidence, but these datasets differ in coverage, resolution, reliability, and interpretation.

GeoThermAI proposes to integrate these sources into a reproducible decision-support workflow.

**The problem is not simply a lack of maps. It is a lack of sufficiently validated, uncertainty-aware, decision-oriented screening tools.**

Whether this gap exists to the extent proposed in Thailand must be established through a review of existing national systems and research.

## 3. Proposed Solution

GeoThermAI will:

1. Harmonize relevant geothermal exploration datasets.
    
2. Build interpretable baseline prospectivity models.
    
3. Test whether additional geospatial and satellite features improve performance.
    
4. Compare simpler and more complex machine-learning approaches.
    
5. Apply geographically appropriate validation.
    
6. Quantify uncertainty and identify insufficient-data areas.
    
7. Produce ranked exploration screening maps.
    
8. Explore scenario-based economic and energy-system implications.
    

## 4. Core Research Questions

**RQ1:** Can multi-source geospatial machine learning improve geothermal exploration screening relative to simpler geological baselines?

**RQ2:** Do satellite-derived features provide measurable additional predictive value?

**RQ3:** Does increased model complexity produce better generalization to geographically unseen areas?

**RQ4:** Can uncertainty-aware rankings improve simulated exploration resource allocation relative to conventional screening approaches?

## 5. Hypotheses

- H1: Selected multi-source features may improve spatial generalization compared with a geology-only baseline.
    
- H2: Complex models will not necessarily outperform simpler baselines.
    
- H3: Uncertainty-aware screening may improve the robustness of exploration prioritization.
    

All hypotheses require empirical testing. None is an established result.

## 6. Intended Impact

### Economic

Improve early-stage exploration prioritization and potentially reduce inefficient allocation of survey budgets.

**Not yet proven:** Any actual monetary savings.

### Environmental and Energy

Support investigation of geothermal resources as a possible low-carbon, potentially continuous energy source.

**Not yet proven:** Commercially viable resources or electricity-generation capacity at any newly ranked location.

### National

Support evidence-informed geothermal resource screening and possible future energy planning in Thailand.

### Scientific

Evaluate the practical contribution of data fusion, model complexity, spatial validation, and uncertainty estimation.

## 7. Project Architecture

**Core research system**

- Geospatial data preparation
    
- Geothermal prospectivity modeling
    
- Model comparison
    
- Spatial validation
    
- Uncertainty estimation
    
- Exploration prioritization
    

**Integrated research component**

- Satellite data fusion and ablation experiments
    

**Supporting features**

- Exploration risk and economic scenario analysis
    
- Energy-system and clean-firm-power scenarios
    
- Interactive geospatial dashboard
    

The supporting features must not compromise the validity or completion of the core research.

## 8. Minimum Viable Research Product

The MVP should contain:

- One defensible pilot study area
    
- A documented dataset and provenance register
    
- At least one simple baseline model
    
- At least one alternative model
    
- Spatially appropriate out-of-sample evaluation
    
- An ablation study for added data sources
    
- A prospectivity screening map
    
- An uncertainty or data-support map
    
- A concise limitations statement
    
- A reproducible analysis workflow
    

An interactive dashboard is desirable but cannot substitute for validation.

## 9. Out of Scope for Initial MVP

Unless suitable evidence becomes available, do not promise:

- Accurate prediction of deep reservoir temperature
    
- Discovery of commercially exploitable geothermal reservoirs
    
- Verified drilling success probabilities
    
- Site-specific proven electricity generation in MW
    
- Bankable levelized cost of electricity
    
- Confirmed exploration cost reductions
    
- National-scale comprehensive resource certification
